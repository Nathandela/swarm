swarm · android launcher icon · six candidates

The prompt that is also an S

“A S with a slightly wiggling bar after it (like the design of a terminal, using the green elegant color of the app) and add a bar on top of the > that gives a S vibe, and the _ is slightly swarmy.”

The brief, verbatim

Every candidate below is the same two marks seen two ways. The S is a three-leg zigzag whose cap is the chevron's own bottom stroke repeated above it; the lower two legs are the terminal prompt's >, and the mark after them is its cursor. The double reading is the design. A mark you have to be told the S is in has failed, and so has one that is just an S standing next to a cursor.

The palette is fixed and comes from the shipped tokens, not from taste: hero green #53CE7C, hero ink #06150C, app ground #08090A. No candidate introduces a colour that is not already in internal/design/tokens.json. All six are cut rather than drawn: flat butt caps, mitred joins, no rounding anywhere, so the reversals are points and the diagonal terminals come off at a blade angle.

The test

What decides it is 48dp, not 512

  • As drawn The full 108dp adaptive canvas, which is the file, not the icon. Nothing here is what anyone sees.
  • As launched The centre 72dp that a launcher actually displays, masked to a circle and to a squircle, drawn at true 48dp. This is the home screen.
  • At the pixel That same 48dp render, magnified without resampling. Detail that merges here is detail the design does not have.
  • Safe zone Only the centre 66dp circle survives every launcher mask. All six measure clear of it; the toggle draws the boundary on every canvas.

Prompt Cap

prompt-cap.svg Survives

The brief taken literally. One weight throughout, and the whole three-leg zigzag folded into the y 38–70 box the bare > occupied on its own, so the S inherits the prompt's proportions instead of growing to fit a letter. The cursor is the wiggle made square: one bar with a single step in it, which is what a terminal cursor mid-blink looks like rather than what a brush stroke looks like.

Trail

trail-echo.svg Marginal

The prompt-first pole, reached by weight rather than by geometry. The chevron carries the heaviest stroke and its cap the lightest — the same diagonal, echoed thinner above — so the eye lands on the > and the S assembles a beat later. The cursor is a block and one shorter echo: the swarm as motion rather than as multiplicity. The cost is that the lightest stroke is also the one doing the S, and at 4.5 units it lands at roughly 2px on a 48dp screen.

Solid Wedge

solid-wedge.svg Survives

The S-first pole: the same zigzag at the heaviest weight the set allows, so what survives downscaling is mass rather than detail. The weight is judged rather than maximal — at 8.5 units the three legs still hold an open channel between them at 48dp, which is the thing that fails first when a mark like this is pushed heavier. Its cursor is the one filled shape on the page, which is what keeps it reading as a cursor beside a stroked letter.

Cell Swarm

cell-swarm.svg Survives

The swarm as multiplicity instead of motion. The underscore is three terminal cells of different sizes scattered off the baseline. This was always the blocky candidate, so sharpening it mostly meant thinning the zigzag into the same weight class as the cells beside it. It bets more on small detail than anything else here, and the bet pays — the cells sit 4.5 units apart and all three still read as squares at 48dp.

Green Tile

green-tile.svg Survives

The only container, and the only inversion. Hero green becomes the ground and the mark is knocked out of it in hero ink, which buys more contrast than anything else in this palette can. Ink cut out of green carries further than green on near-black, so this reads as the heaviest of the thin candidates without being the heaviest drawn. The rounded square is there only so the shape stands alone; on device the launcher supplies the mask and the background layer is a flat green fill.

Wide Rule

wide-rule.svg Reads, but

The one that changes the composition rather than the details. Nothing follows the chevron at all; the swarm is a dashed rule running the full width underneath, so the prompt sits on a command line made of many cells. It gives the set its only stacked silhouette and the dashes are coarse enough to stay plural at 48dp. It pays for that composition twice: it is the thinnest mark in the set, so the S turns spindly at 48dp, and with no cursor after the > the rule reads as a progress bar under a logo rather than as a terminal line.

The verdict

Two survive the small-size test

Solid Wedge

The only one whose silhouette still carries at arm's length. Weight is what survives downscaling, and at 8.5 units it is heavy without fusing: the channels between the three legs stay open and the stepped cursor keeps its step.

Green Tile

The clearest S at 48dp by some margin, because contrast is doing work no stroke weight can. It is also the only candidate that holds its own against a bright wallpaper rather than disappearing into a dark one.

Why those two and not the others. Prompt Cap and Cell Swarm both survive — nothing merges, the cells stay three — they simply have less presence than the two above at the same size. Trail is the one to watch: its cap leg is 4.5 units, roughly 2px on a 48dp screen, and that leg is the entire difference between an S and a bare chevron, so the lightest stroke in the set is carrying the heaviest idea. Wide Rule is the thinnest mark here and spends its cursor on the composition, which costs it the prompt reading twice over.

Green Tile is a treatment as much as a candidate. Its zigzag is the same letterform as the others; what differs is the figure/ground. That makes this two decisions rather than one — which mark, then green-on-dark or ink-on-green — and the tile only answers the second. It can be applied to whichever mark wins.

Two things worth fixing before anyone commits. First, the mitred joins reach further than the files think: measured from rendered pixels, Solid Wedge sits at 32.4dp and Cell Swarm at 32.2dp against a 33dp safe circle, where their own comments claim 30.3 and 28.7. Both are still legal, but Solid Wedge is inside its margin by 0.6dp, which is not the margin those comments promise. Second, the S can be misread — a lightning bolt at worst, a numeral 5 at a glance. What disambiguates it is the top leg running clear to the right of the reversal below it, so any future tightening should not shorten that leg. If the S ever has to be explained, the design has lost the thing it was for.

Colophon

Candidates
docs/design/icon-candidates/ — six standalone SVGs, each commented with its concept and its token sources. The safe-zone radius quoted in each file is the author's estimate; the figures on this page are measured from rendered pixels and are the ones to trust where the two disagree.
Palette source
internal/design/tokens.json and android/app/src/main/res/values/colors.xml. Three colours are used across all six: p-hero #53CE7C, p-hero-ink #06150C, p-bg #08090A. Nothing outside that file appears in any candidate.
Geometry
108dp adaptive canvas, matching the conventions in ic_launcher_foreground.xml. Safe-zone figures are measured from rendered pixels, not estimated: every mark's furthest drawn point, stroke included, against the 33dp radius the launcher mask guarantees.
Not touched
The shipped icon. Nothing under android/app/src/main/res/ was modified — these are candidates to choose between, and adopting one is a separate change that also needs the monochrome and themed-icon treatments checked.
