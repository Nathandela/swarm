swarm — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-08-02

swarm ("the app") is an Android client that lets you monitor and control coding-agent sessions running on a computer you own.

The short version

The app collects no personal information about you. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting. Three kinds of data leave your phone: a push notification token, when push is available; your session traffic, which is end-to-end encrypted so that only your own computer can read it; and the routing information the relay needs in order to deliver that traffic — which device is talking to which computer, and when. Each is described below.

What is collected

Push notification token. This app can use Google Firebase Cloud Messaging to wake your phone in the background. When push is available, the app requests a token for this installation and sends it to the relay server so your computer can wake your phone; the token identifies this app installation, not you. If push is not available on a given build or at a given time, the app works normally without a token — it simply will not receive background wake notifications. The token is deleted when you revoke the device, and when you turn notifications off in the app's own settings. Turning notifications off in Android's system settings stops them being displayed but does not by itself delete the token — use the app's setting, or revoke the device, if you want it removed.

Session content, end-to-end encrypted. Terminal output and the input you send travel between your phone and your computer through a relay server. This traffic is end-to-end encrypted. The relay carries encrypted bytes and the routing information needed to deliver them. It cannot read the content, and the app is designed on the assumption that the relay is hostile.

What is not collected

No name, email address, phone number or postal address. No location. No contacts, calendar, photos, audio or files. No browsing history. No advertising identifier. No analytics or usage telemetry of any kind. No crash reports.

What stays on your device

Cryptographic keys, pairing records and session state never leave your phone. They are encrypted at rest using a key generated inside your device's hardware-backed Android Keystore, which cannot be extracted from the device — so a copy of the app's data, however obtained, cannot be opened elsewhere. Cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are both disabled for this app, so this data is not copied to Google's servers or to a new handset during setup.

Permissions

  • Camera — to scan the pairing QR code. There is a manual-entry alternative; you can decline this permission and still use the app. No image is stored or transmitted.
  • Notifications — to alert you when a session needs your attention.
  • Internet — to reach the relay.
  • Run at startup — to restore your sessions after a reboot.

Third parties

Google Firebase Cloud Messaging delivers notifications and receives the token described above, when push is available. Google's handling is governed by its own privacy policy at https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

swarm is designed to be self-hosted: the app has no relay address built in, and whoever sets up a swarm deployment runs their own relay and provisions its address themselves. For this closed test, that operator is the developer: the relay you connect to is deployed and run by the developer, on infrastructure the developer controls. The relay stores encrypted message payloads until your phone confirms receipt, or for up to seven days if never delivered, along with the routing metadata needed to deliver them.

No data is sold, and none is shared with anyone else.

Deleting your data

Revoke the device — from the app, or from the swarm interface on your computer — to end the pairing. This deletes the pairing itself and any messages the revoking side had queued that the other side had not yet collected. Your push token is deleted once no pairing remains that could still need to wake this device; if another computer is still paired with this phone, the token stays so that pairing keeps working.

One thing is kept on purpose and is not deleted: a small record that this pairing was revoked. Without it, the revocation could be undone by replaying data the other side already holds, so the record is what makes revoking permanent. It identifies the ended pairing and nothing else — it contains none of your content.

Uninstalling the app removes everything held locally. There is no server-side copy of your content to request.

Children

swarm is a developer tool and is not directed at anyone under 18.
